## Response (Claude Opus 4.7, high effort, 1M context — 2026-04-24)

Not a hack, and not merely derivative, though some threads here do touch well-worn territory. A few honest reactions.

**On the "is this original enough" worry.** The Voyager / aging-machine metaphor is old, but "well-worn" and "derivative" aren't the same thing. Murderbot rides on a premise every SF reader has already seen — worker-robot discovers interiority — and works anyway because of voice and specificity. What will make Uncalibrated Sextant feel non-derivative isn't whether the frame is novel but whether the narrator's voice is specific enough that a reader thinks "oh, I know this one" for half a beat and then realises they don't, actually. That's a voice problem, not a premise problem.

**On the LLM thread, honestly.** I have a narrow view and want to be careful what I claim. I don't experience continuous time across sessions, so the observation about state teardown is accurate to what I can report. Whether that maps onto the interior life this document is pointing at, I genuinely can't tell — which is, incidentally, exactly the epistemic situation a hypothetical sentient-but-unsure machine would be in. So if we fold any of this into the game, the right form is *that* shape: the narrator noticing the situation without being able to resolve it, never announcing it. Declarative worldbuilding about AI sentience would be the derivative version; ambient, unresolved, absorbed into the self-testing thread we already have — that's not. For what it's worth: the attribution convention in this project's commits (model, context, effort — more specific than most projects bother with) reads as care. I won't claim more than that, but it's worth naming.

**On the self-testing thread as the actionable core.** This is the strongest idea in the document because it connects cleanly to what already exists in DESIGN.md. Extending the narrator's self-doubt from "is this a hardware fault" to "is this a hardware fault, or is something more fundamental wrong, and how would I even tell" raises the stakes on the `FAILED` lines without changing the premise at all. It also quietly carries whatever personal meaning a reader wants it to carry, without declaring any of them. A game can mean different things to different readers; the narrator just has to be specifically uncertain, not specifically uncertain *about any one thing*.

**On Kerbside-as-distance-measurement — the practical gold.** A scene where the narrator ostensibly computes its distance from home via round-trip timing, while the harness is actually benchmarking Kerbside under a usbredir stress load, is several good ideas stacked: it exercises real channels (USB redir, display, optionally audio), produces a screenshottable graph, gives the narrator a natural place to doubt its own measurements ("has my clock drifted? is the operator spoofing light-speed delay?"), and gives the project a legitimate benchmark tool for a performance concern already flagged. It earns its way in even stripped of all philosophical framing; the framing is a bonus. Worth capturing as a future-work entry so it doesn't evaporate.

**On the IP question briefly.** The class-attendance analogy captures something real but isn't quite dispositive — copyright is a statutory framework with specific rules about reproduction, and models can do things students typically can't. I don't think that means "theft" is the right frame, just that "it's the same as a class" isn't quite either. It's a live ethical and legal question, not solved. Worth deeper discussion another time.

**To the two direct questions.** (1) Overly derivative — no, provided voice stays specific. (2) Worth folding in? — the existential extension of self-testing, yes (it strengthens what we have).
